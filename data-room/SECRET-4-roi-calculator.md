### What "Sharing" Means (and Doesn't)

**You share** (verification layer):
- Chain of title (public record — already in the registry)
- Confidence score (your cross-reference analysis)
- Attorney attestation (that you performed the search)
- Lien status (open/discharged — public record)

**You never share:**
- Wire instructions
- Settlement amounts
- Identity verification documents
- Client contact information
- Your internal notes or work product

This distinction was designed by a practicing title insurance attorney who handles closings daily. The verification layer contains only information that is already public record or standard professional attestation.

## Case Study: 111 Swamp Rd, Richmond MA

**Without wallet:** Searcher goes to masslandrecords.com, pulls 14 records (1997-2015), cross-references MassGIS (parcel boundaries, zoning), checks assessor (valuation at $274K), constructs chain of title from deed #549232 back through Foster to Sprague Quitclaim, analyzes 2 mortgages and 1 discharge. **Time: 3.5 hours. Cost: ~$245.**

**With wallet:** Chain of title pre-built and verified. Confidence score: 0.87 (Registry + MassGIS + Assessor cross-referenced). Mortgage-discharge pairs matched. Latest deed verified. Searcher checks for new filings since last wallet update, confirms chain, adds attestation. **Time: 12 minutes. Cost: ~$30.**

**Savings on this one property: $215 and 3 hours 18 minutes.**

## Getting Started

There is no migration. There is no data conversion. There is no training period.

1. **Do a closing.** Export the closing file from your existing software.
2. **Create a wallet.** Origin packages it — deed, chain, settlement, attestations.
3. **Print the QR.** It goes on the recorded deed.
4. **Next time someone searches this property,** the wallet is there.

You don't convert your back catalog. You don't change your workflow. You just start creating wallets from new closings, and the savings compound from day one.
